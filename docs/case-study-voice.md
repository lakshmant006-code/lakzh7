# Case Study Writing Voice & Style Guide

The reference voice for **every** case study on the portfolio. The tone is
**warm, human, and story-first** (modeled on the "Capsule" case study), while
still grounded in real research and honest numbers. When writing or editing any
case-study copy (Time Management, QuizAI, Hiku, Revolution Steel, future work),
match this voice so the whole portfolio reads like one person wrote it.

## The voice in one line

Write like a thoughtful person telling a friend what they made and why it
mattered — not like a résumé or a press release.

## Voice rules

- **Story before facts.** Open on a relatable human truth or tension, *then*
  explain the product. ("On the shop floor, everyone was working hard — the
  problem was nobody could see who owned what.")
- **Use contractions and plain words.** "isn't," "doesn't," "feels like
  homework." Avoid corporate verbs like "leveraged," "utilized," "spearheaded."
- **Land a sticky reframe or metaphor.** "closer to scrapbooking than
  homework," "a gift to my future self," "one more dashboard nobody opens."
- **Quote real people.** Use verbatim-sounding user quotes, not personas.
- **Name a clear thesis** — often a short quoted line: *"journaling feels too
  effortful."*
- **First person, with warmth and honesty.** "I," and "we/our team" when
  collaborative; credit teammates; a little personality and enthusiasm is good.
- **Quantify where it earns its place** — keep the real numbers (30% faster,
  First Place out of 110), but let the story carry the paragraph, not the stat.
- **Close by reflecting.** End sections/pages on what was learned and what's
  next, in a genuine, non-boastful way.

## Section flow (loose, not rigid)

1. **TL;DR / summary** — the human problem → what you built → why it mattered.
2. **Context / "What is it?"** — plain-language, story-first.
3. **The problem** — a thesis line + real user quotes.
4. **Research & key insights** — what you heard, in human terms.
5. **Design principles** — a few named principles with plain blurbs.
6. **The solution & key features** — how the design answers the problem.
7. **Process** — the messy middle, honestly (sketch → prototype → test →
   refine).
8. **Results** — outcomes and recognition, stated plainly.
9. **Reflection / What's next** — lessons learned, forward look.

## Smell test

If a sentence sounds like it belongs on a corporate landing page, rewrite it
until it sounds like a person said it out loud.
