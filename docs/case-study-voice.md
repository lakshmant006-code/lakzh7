# Case Study Writing Voice & Style Guide

The reference voice for **every** case study on the portfolio. Derived from the
internship-experience research writeup. When writing or editing any case study
copy (Time Management, QuizAI, Hiku, Revolution Steel, and any future project),
match this flow, structure, and tone so the whole portfolio reads as one voice.

## Section flow (the order each case study narrative follows)

1. **Summary** — aim of the project → the research approach in one line →
   quantified outcomes → a sentence on my role and why it was pivotal.
2. **About the Client** — short; keep the client abstract/anonymous for
   confidentiality; describe them by caliber and scale ("a global team of
   strategists, UX/CX designers, developers…").
3. **About the Project** — first-person mandate ("Tasked with…, I led…"),
   the personal strengths I leveraged, and the overarching goal.
4. **Process** — one lead sentence naming the methods, then a bulleted list.
   Each bullet = **Named method + the tool** ("In-Depth Interviews in FigJam")
   → what I did → a concrete number (12 interviews, 90% participation, 2 weeks).
5. **Key Findings & Impact** — each finding is an **outcome-named heading**
   ("Standardized Training Process"), a short paragraph stating what was built
   and the quantified impact, then **Problem statement** + **Solution**
   (the Solution restates the headline metric).
6. **Additional Requirements & Approach** — collaboration, confidentiality,
   organizational alignment, continuous feedback loops.
7. **Key Contributions** — terse first-person, past-tense verbs (Led,
   Conducted, Facilitated, Introduced, Presented), each carrying a number.

## Voice rules

- **First person, active, past tense**: "I led," "I conducted," "I advocated."
- **Quantify everything.** Every claim carries a number, and the headline
  metric is repeated in the summary, the finding, and the solution.
- **Outcome-first framing.** Name findings by the result achieved, not the
  activity performed.
- **Problem → Solution pairing.** Every impact area states an explicit problem,
  then a concrete solution.
- **Name the tools** (Figma, FigJam, Condens, Theydo, VS Code…) to signal
  fluency and method rigor.
- **Confident, advocacy tone**: "pivotal," "crucial," "advocated for the
  interns' needs." I position myself as a leader and a user advocate.
- **Keep clients confidential**; describe them by their strengths and scale.
- **Highlight methodological initiative** ("introduced a new methodology to
  quantitatively track…") to show I bring new approaches, not just execution.

## Signature metrics pattern

- Percentage improvements: "reduced onboarding time by 30%", "37% increase in
  satisfaction."
- Counts: "12 in-depth interviews", "90% participation rate", "over 2 weeks."
- Always tie each metric to the specific change that produced it.
